\begin{abstract}
Deep learning models for electroencephalography (EEG) pathology detection achieve competitive accuracy but offer limited clinical interpretability. We introduce concept bottleneck models (CBMs) for EEG pathology detection, which
use clinical EEG biomarkers computed analytically from the signal as a learning bottleneck to be used for prediction. Those concepts include band power, spectral ratios, alpha peak frequency and amplitude, hemispheric asymmetry, and phase locking values. Using a shallow convolutional neural network (CNN) backbone, we compare a black-box baseline with a hybrid CBM and the classifier receives a residual projection of the backbone features. The other variant is a pure CBM where the classifier receives only the predicted concepts. Models are evaluated on abnormality detection (TUAB) and dementia classification (CAUEEG)
across three seeds followed by concept prediction quality, concept intervention and leakage analysis. Both CBMs approach the baseline on TUAB ($82.7$--$82.9\%$ vs $83.6\%$) and are $2$--$5$ percentage points lower on CAUEEG ($57.9$--$60.5\%$ vs $62.7\%$). The pure CBM exhibits concept leakage where the predicted concepts deviate from their ground truth analytical values, and replacing them with their true values reduces accuracy by $26$ and
$22$ points on TUAB and CAUEEG. The leakage is mitigated by increasing the weight of the concept loss which helps to reduce this intervention gap to
$7$ and $4$ percentage points and raising mean concept $R^2$ to $0.53$ and $0.64$ with small accuracy cost on TUAB but a substantial cost on CAUEEG. The code of the experiments and full logs  are available at
\href{https://www.github.com/mhmdrdwn/cbm}{https://www.github.com/mhmdrdwn/cbm}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Electroencephalography (EEG) provides a non invasive and cost effective tool used in clinical screening settings for neurodegenerative diseases. Machine Learning has advanced substantially in the analysis of EEG data with various neural networks architectures. Those networks such as Convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and graph neural networks (GNNs) achieving competitive performances on large clinical corpora, as demonstrated by the representative studies
\cite{schirrmeister2017}, \cite{lawhern2016eegnet}, \cite{demir2021eeg},\cite{song2018eeg},\cite{klepl2024}, \cite{roy2019chrononet}.

Despite the accuracy gains, interpretability of those models remains limited. This interpretation has to be clinically viable which remains a challenge in clinical applications. In this settings, control is essential for safety and regulatory compliance.

Current EEG interpretability methods are mainly Post-hoc attribution methods such as saliency maps \cite{simonyan2013}, GradCAM \cite{selvaraju2017} and SHAPley values \cite{lundberg2017}. Those methods explain decisions of the models after the predictions are made but cannot be verified or corrected at inference time. Using this approach, input features are highlighted using attention weights or learned saliencies \cite{demir2022, zhao2024}. Other studies are breaking EEG signals into frequency band features and using them in building and interpreting GNNs \cite{tawhid2024exploring}, \cite{radwan2025frequency}, \cite{klepl2024graph}, \cite{abadal2025graph}.

Concept Bottleneck Models (CBMs) \cite{koh2020} offer a new direction for explaining Deep Learning models. The internal encoding layers of the model are constrained by a set of predefined human interpretable concepts and the final classifier operates on these concepts. In this setting, clinicians can inspect and verify those concepts and intervene in it by correcting a concept at inference without retraining. CBMs train models to predict human interpretable concepts as intermediate representations and use them for predictions. However, these methods are challenging and one of those challenges is the information leakage \cite{havasi2022}. This happens when the concept layer encodes task-relevant information that bypasses the concept definitions \cite{mahinpei2021}. CBMs have not been applied specifically in the domain of EEG data, to the best of our knowledge. In this article, we explore the use of CBMs in the EEG domain. 

The EEG domain is particularly well suited for the CBMs method. Clinical EEG concepts such as delta band power, theta/alpha ratio, and alpha peak frequency are precisely defined analytical quantities. This means that those concepts are computable directly from raw signals using well defined neurophysiological formulas. This means it provides computed concept labels for every EEG record without any manual annotation which can be an advantage over other applications that require manual concept annotation. 

In this article, we introduce different variants of CBMs for EEG pathology detection and make the following contributions:


\begin{enumerate}

\item We introduce the first application of Concept Bottleneck Models (CBMs) to EEG pathology detection using clinical EEG concepts that are computed analytically from the signal. The approach is evaluated on two clinically distinct tasks which are abnormality detection (TUAB dataset) and dementia classification (CAUEEG dataset).

\item We show that accuracy alone does not reveal whether concept explanations are faithful. We examined leakage analysis for the used models where predicted concepts deviate from their analytical values and use hidden label information to make predictions. 

\item We show that increasing the weight of the concept loss weight substantially reduces concept leakage. Furthermore, we report the accuracy cost of this mitigation method as it exposes a trade-off between concept faithfulness and classification accuracy.

\end{enumerate}



\section{Data}
\label{sec:dataset}
Two datasets are used in this study. The first is the TUAB dataset which is used for the task of abnormality detection while the other is the CAUEEG which is used for dementia detection. 

\subsubsection{Temple University Hospital Abnormal EEG Corpus (TUAB)}
\hfill \break
The TUAB dataset \cite{obeid2016} has $2,993$ recordings from $2,329$ subjects, split into $2,717$ training and $276$ test data samples. The test set contains $126$ abnormal and $150$ normal recordings. Labels indicate whether a record shows clear evidence of pathology which is interpreted as abnormal. Abnormality means clinical pathology, brain dysfunction or neurological disorders. The data samples are recorded using the standard $10-20$ montage. This system has $19$ electrodes plus two reference electrodes (A1, A2). The electrodes are Fp1, Fp2, F7, F3, Fz, F4, F8, T3, C3, Cz, C4, T4, T5, P3, Pz, P4, T6, O1, O2, A1, A2. In these experiments, signals are resampled to $100$ Hz and bandpass filtered between $0.5$ and $45$ Hz using Butterworth filter. Clinical concept computation (section~\ref{sec:concepts}) uses only the $19$ electrodes in which we exclude the reference electrodes A1, A2. 


\subsubsection{Chung-Ang University Hospital EEG (CAUEEG)}
\hfill \break
CAUEEG dataset \cite{kim2023caueeg} was collected at Chung-Ang University Hospital in South Korea. It contains $1,379$ EEG recordings from $1,155$ unique subjects. The subjects are categorized into four clinical groups: $459$ healthy controls (NC), $417$ with mild cognitive impairment (MCI), $311$ with dementia, and $192$ with other neurological conditions. The diagnoses of Dementia in the data samples follow protocols from the National Institute of Neurological and Communicative Disorders and Stroke and Alzheimer’s Disease and Related Disorders Association and the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders. Similar to TUAB, the samples used the $10-20$ system at $200$ Hz and filtered with a $0.5$:$70$ Hz bandpass filter. In these experiments, we evaluate on the CAUEEG-Dementia task which is a three-class classification of NC, MCI, and dementia. This task is more clinically demanding according to the original authors \cite{kim2023caueeg}. Similar to original authors \cite{kim2023caueeg}, our experimental split uses $950$ samples for training, $119$ for validation, and $118$ for testing. The signals are resampled to $100$ Hz and bandpass filtered between $0.5$ and $45$ Hz.


\section{Methods}
\subsection{Clinical EEG Concepts}
\label{sec:concepts}

In the experiments, we use $30$ candidate clinical concepts that are computed analytically from raw EEG signals using established neurophysiological formulas. Those concepts are deterministically computed from every EEG record without the need for manual annotation. All concepts are normalized using the training set. The computed concepts are $20$ band power, three hemispheric asymmetry, two power spectral ratio, two based on peak amplitude and frequency, and three PLV connectivity. The details of computing those concepts are shown in the following sections.


\subsubsection{Regional band power ($20$ Concepts)}
\hfill \break
Those $20$ concepts are computed from the signals using the band power of the EEG channels after breaking the signals into frequency bands. Band power or Power Spectrum Density (PSD) is calculated per channel using Welch's method \cite{welch1967} with $512$ as the length of each segment. PSD is the average of the squared magnitudes of Short Time Fourier Transform over overlapping segments. The value is transformed to log scale for numerical stability. Five brain regions are used from the EEG $10$-$20$ montage ($19$ used electrodes). Those electrodes are frontal (Fp1, Fp2, F3, F4, Fz, F7, F8), temporal (T3, T4, T5, T6), central (C3, Cz, C4), parietal (P3, Pz, P4) and occipital (O1, O2). The regional band power concept is the mean of PSD across all electrodes in that region. Four frequency bands are used: delta ($0.5$:$4$ Hz), theta ($4$:$8$Hz), alpha ($8$:$13$Hz), and beta ($13$:$30$Hz). This yields $20$ concepts from the five regions and four frequency bands. The concepts such as delta and theta bandpower have been studied for their clinical relevance \cite{niedermeyer2011}, \cite{lin2021differences}, \cite{nardone2018usefulness}, \cite{tomasello2023neuropsychological}. Reduced occipital alpha was studied as a general marker of cortical dysfunction \cite{klimesch1999}. Lower alpha band activity was found in dementia patients compared to controls in the orbital frontal and temporal lobes \cite{nishida2011differences}. 


\subsubsection{Hemispheric asymmetry indices (3 concepts)}
\hfill \break
Asymmetry index quantifies the relative difference in alpha power ($P^{\alpha}$) between the right and left hemispheres for a given region.

\begin{equation}
  \text{Asymmetry} =
  \frac{P_\text{right}^{\alpha} - P_\text{left}^{\alpha}}
       {P_\text{right}^{\alpha} + P_\text{left}^{\alpha}}
  \label{eq:asymmetry}
\end{equation}

Given equation \ref{eq:asymmetry}, values range from $-1$ (left dominance) to $+1$ (right dominance) and zero indicates hemispheric symmetry. Three electrodes pairs define the three asymmetry concepts which are frontal (F4 vs F3), temporal (T4 vs T3), and posterior (O2 vs O1). The study \cite{mizrak2024investigation} reports the difference of hemispheric asymmetry between Alzheimer and control groups. Additionally, Frontal alpha asymmetry is also an established marker studied in depression \cite{davidson2004}, \cite{peng2026altered}, \cite{jia2026hemispheric}.


\subsubsection{Spectral ratio markers (2 concepts)}
\hfill \break
Spectral ratios quantify the balance between slow and fast waves powers. This is done by dividing the mean of slow waves power over the mean of fast waves power of all electrodes. Two ratios are defined as follows:

\textbf{Theta/Alpha Ratio (TAR):}
\begin{equation}
  \text{TAR} =
  \frac{\bar{P}^{\theta}}{\bar{P}^{\alpha}}
  \label{eq:tar}
\end{equation}

where $\bar{P}^{\theta}$ and $\bar{P}^{\alpha}$ are the global mean theta and alpha powers across all $19$ electrodes. Higher TAR indicates increased cognitive load and mental fatigue and is highlighted in Alzheimer groups \cite{wascher2014frontal}, \cite{borghini2014measuring}, \cite{olugun2024quantitative}.

DTABR is also another slow-vs-fast index given as the following:

\textbf{Delta+Theta over Alpha+Beta Ratio (DTABR):}
\begin{equation}
  \text{DTABR} =
  \frac{\bar{P}^{\delta} + \bar{P}^{\theta}}
       {\bar{P}^{\alpha} + \bar{P}^{\beta}}
  \label{eq:dtabr}
\end{equation}

DTABR index measures the balance between the power of slow and fast wave and is used in the studies including \cite{olivia2024qeeg}, \cite{hussain2022quantitative}. 


\subsubsection{Spectral structure concepts (2 concepts)}
\hfill \break
Two concepts are used in this family. The first is Alpha peak frequency (APF) which is the dominant oscillation frequency within the alpha band. It is computed as the frequency of maximum power spectral density in the alpha band range for the occipital region. Some studies examined the occipital dominant rhythm is reduced in dementia and MCI \cite{klimesch1999}, \cite{radwan2025frequency}, \cite{tawhid2024exploring}.


The other concept is the Alpha peak amplitude (APA) which is the maximum PSD value within the alpha range over occipital electrodes. As APF tells what frequency is the dominant alpha rhythm at, APA tells how strong it is. This value is log transformed for stability. A strong occipital alpha peak indicates an intact dominant rhythm where the absence indicates abnormality \cite{labonte2023posterior}, \cite{olejarczyk2017eeg}.


\subsubsection{Phase Locking Value (3 concepts):}
\hfill \break

Phase Locking Value (PLV) is widely used for measuring the functional connectivity between brain regions. The asymmetry indices expressed earlier in equation~\ref{eq:asymmetry} quantify which hemisphere has more alpha power. The focus here is tell whether the two hemispheres oscillate in phase. We compute the PLV between the same three electrode pairs used for the asymmetry concepts (frontal, temporal, posterior).

\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{PLV} = \left| \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T} e^{i(\phi_\text{right}(t) - \phi_\text{left}(t))} \right|
  \label{eq:plv}
\end{equation}

where $\phi_\text{right}(t)$ and $\phi_\text{left}(t)$ are the instantaneous phases (obtained via Hilbert transform) of the alpha filtered signals at the right and left electrode of the pair. PLV ranges from $0$ which means no phase relationship to $1$ which means phase locked.

\subsubsection{Neural network backbone}
\hfill \break
The Shallow CNN in this study architecture (after \cite{schirrmeister2017}) applies temporal convolution ($40$ filters with kernel size $25$), spatial convolution spanning all $n$ electrodes ($40$ filters, kernel size $n \times 1$), batch normalization, square, average pooling and log transformation.

\subsubsection{Concept bottleneck}
\label{sec:concept_bottleneck}
\hfill \break


The backbone output is averaged over time produces a $d$-dimensional embedding $\mathbf{z}$ with one value per filter. A two-layer MLP (Linear, ReLU, Dropout, Linear) followed by a sigmoid $\sigma$ maps $\mathbf{z}$ to maps to $N_c$ predicted concept values between $0$ and $1$.

\begin{equation}
  \hat{\mathbf{c}} = \sigma(\mathrm{MLP}(\mathbf{z})) \in [0,1]^{N_c}
  \label{eq:concepts}
\end{equation}

We evaluate two variants of the bottleneck models for predictions. In the hybrid variant (Figure \ref{fig:architecture2}), the classifier combines the concept predictions and the residual pathway which is a projection of the raw backbone embedding $\mathbf{z}$ that bypasses the concept layer entirely as in the following:

\begin{equation}
  \hat{y} = \mathrm{Linear}([\hat{\mathbf{c}};\, \mathrm{ReLU}(W_r\mathbf{z})])
  \label{eq:classifier}
\end{equation}

where $\mathrm{ReLU}(W_r\mathbf{z})$ is the residual projection. In the pure variant (Figure \ref{fig:architecture1}), the residual term is removed and the classifier sees only the concept vector:

\begin{equation}
  \hat{y} = \mathrm{Linear}(\hat{\mathbf{c}})
  \label{eq:classifier_plain}
\end{equation}

The two variants share every parameter except $W_r$ and the input representation of the classifier. The main difference between them is the effect of the residual pathway. The parameter counts for all variants are reported in table~\ref{tab:params}.

\subsection{Concept Quality Validation}
\label{sec:validation}

As we use the model to predict those precomputed $30$ concepts (section \ref{sec:concept_bottleneck}), the coefficient of determination ($R^2$) between model predicted and the computed concept values on a held-out validation subset is measured. Here $R^2$ measures how well the concept predictor can predict the analytically computed concept value from the model learned features. The $30$ concepts are used as supervised targets during training and are all visible to the classifier. Post training, the $30$ concepts are used for evaluation as shown in section \ref{sec:concept_quality} and intervention as shown in section \ref{sec:intervention}. The aim is to decide which concepts are reliable enough to report or intervene.


\subsection{Training}

The models in these experiments are trained with the joint loss:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cls}}(\hat{y}, y) +
                \lambda\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{concept}}(\hat{\mathbf{c}},
                \mathbf{c})
  \label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cls}}$ is cross entropy loss and $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{concept}}$ is Mean Square Error (MSE) loss between predicted and the computed concept values, and $\lambda = 0.5$. Further use of $\lambda = 5, 20$ is applied to strengthen the concept loss in the training. The choices of $\lambda$ is reasoned about as a leakage mitigation in the section \ref{sec:mitigation}. In these experiments, the Adam optimizer is used with learning rate $10^{-3}$ and weight decay $10^{-4}$ and cosine annealing. Gradient clipping is also applied to limit the exploding gradients. All experiments use the seeds $42$, $43$ and $44$ to test model stability.

The values of $R^2$ are relatively higher in the hybrid than the pure setup. Using the CAUEEG dataset, the same patterns show except the PLV concepts which shows limited $R^2$ value $+0.001$. The band power ($R^2=+0.435$), spectral ratio ($R^2=+0.286$) and spectral structure ($R^2=+0.193$) show relatively higher and positive values. It is noticed that the $R^2$ values is poorer in the pure setup than the hybrid model. Within the band power family, alpha is a reliable predictor across every region on both datasets under the hybrid design (TUAB: $R^2=0.38$--$0.53$, CAUEEG: $R^2=0.39$--$0.71$). Hemispheric asymmetry concepts fail across every configuration in both datasets as the values of  $R^2$ are either negative or near zero. 

The spectral ratio concepts (theta/alpha ratio and DTABR) are reliably positive under the hybrid design on both datasets (TUAB:$R^2=0.348$, CAUEEG:$R^2=0.286$). On the other hand, their behaviour under the pure bottleneck are different. DTABR has negative on both datasets (TUAB:$-0.27$ , CAUEEG:$-0.70$). The theta/alpha ratio is also unstable across seeds.

The results from Section~\ref{sec:results_classification} highlight the presence of the residual pathway has limited effects on changing classification accuracy. The accuracies are $0.827$ and $0.829$ for pure and  the hybrid setups, respectively, using the TUAB dataset. Using the CAUEEG dataset, the accuracies are $0.579$ and $0.605$ ($2.6$ point gap) for pure and the hybrid setups, respectively. This conclusion does not hold in concept prediction quality as the pure setup shows poorer values of $R^2$ in general. This means that the accuracy is not an evidence of the concept quality. The classifiers of both setup can reach the similar accuracy in TUAB and slightly and slightly lower on CAUEEG while the concept predictions are measurably less reliable. This is examined further in sections \ref{sec:leakage} and  \ref{sec:intervention}.

PLV connectivity shows a clear dataset split. On TUAB, PLV are relatively reliable concepts with both variant setup (frontal: $R^2=0.485$ hybrid, $0.141$ pure, temporal: $0.166$ hybrid, $0.117$ pure). On CAUEEG, PLV cocnepts are poorer where none of the three PLV concepts reliably reach $R^2 \geq 0.10$ with both variants. Furthermore, under the CAUEEG pure bottleneck, PLV predictions are even negative on average ($R^2=-0.479$) and the frontal concept is highly unstable across seeds (std $=0.653$). In this CAUEEG settings, PLV concepts are not a stable signals.


\subsection{Leakage analysis}
\label{sec:leakage}

As noticed above, the residual pathway lets the hybrid model use backbone features beyond the predicted concepts. This may lead to the point that predictions are less driven by the concepts. This is known as concept leakage of task relevant features. To quantify this directly, we freeze the trained CB-CNN backbones and train three linear probes on its extracted features. All training is done with identical architecture and training parameters but differing only in input which are concept-only, residual-only and both combined. The results are reported in table \ref{tab:leakage}.

Table~\ref{tab:leakage} shows the results of those three probes. On TUAB, the concept probe ($0.830$) matches the full model ($0.829$) and the residual features does not improve it as the accuracy of combined $0.821$. The residual probe reaches a similar accuracy ($0.819$), so the residual carries largely the same information as the concepts rather than additional information. On CAUEEG, the residual probe ($0.636$) exceeds the concept probe ($0.576$) and the combined probe ($0.630$) improves on the concepts by about five points, indicating that the residual captures task-relevant information that the concept set does not contain. The hybrid concept explanations therefore do not reflect how its decisions are made. So part of the hybrid accuracy on the prediction task cannot be explained by its concepts. This is further mitigated in section~\ref{sec:mitigation}.



\subsection{Concept intervention experiment}
\label{sec:intervention}

In the experiments, we replaced $30$ predicted concepts with its actual computed ground truth value at the inference time and measured the resulting change in test accuracy. The reason for this method is mitigate the low prediction quality of concepts by replacing all of them at inference time. The intervention configuration is run across all seeds ($42$, $43$, $44$) using the saved models.

Table~\ref{tab:intervention} reports the results of the concept intervention experiments. On both datasets, correcting the $30$ concepts at once leaves the hybrid accuracy unchanged. The accuracy changes from $0.829$ to $0.831$ on TUAB and $0.605$ to $0.599$ on CAUEEG. On the other hand, pure model shows different pattern on both datasets. Correcting the $30$ concepts collapses TUAB accuracy from $0.827$ to $0.564$ (change is $-0.263$) and CAUEEG accuracy from $0.579$ to $0.362$ (change $-0.218$). The ground-truth concepts values actively hurt the pure model classifier accuracy. The hybrid design is unaffected by the same intervention is an evidence that its residual pathway, not its concept layer, is doing the actual task relevant learning. This is consistent with  the leakage analysis in section~\ref{sec:leakage}.

\subsection{Leakage mitigation}
\label{sec:mitigation}

Sections~\ref{sec:intervention} and~\ref{sec:leakage} identify two distinct interesting results. The first is connected to the hybrid model residual pathway has learn representation that bypasses the concepts. This is earlier referred as leakage. The other is the concept intervention gap noticed when applied to the pure model. The concept intervention shows its own concept predictions diverge from their true values which matters to its decision. This experiment targets the second observation directly. The term $\lambda$ in equation~\ref{eq:loss} is simply a loss weight. This weight can be used for for matching the concept predictions to their true values in the pure bottleneck model. We retrained the pure model from scratch, across the three seeds on both datasets, at $\lambda \in \{0.5, 5, 20\}$.  The $\lambda=0.5$ is what was used in earlier experiment results reported in sections \ref{sec:results_classification}, \ref{sec:leakage}, \ref{tab:intervention}. For each run of different $\lambda$, measured accuracy, mean concept $R^2$, and the full-concept intervention experiments are run. This experiment is deliberately applied on the  pure bottleneck only. For the hybrid, raising $\lambda$ may improve concept $R^2$ without necessarily without guarantee of residual pathway leakage. This is because the the hybrid classifier used concepts and residual and used cross-entropy for classification .

First, concept $R^2$ rises substantially with increasing $\lambda$. $R^2$ increased from $-0.213$ to $+0.533$ on TUAB, and from $+0.026$ to $+0.639$ on CAUEEG. This argues that $\lambda$ is a correct mitigation for the pure concept fidelity. Second, the intervention gain substantially reduced with increasing $\lambda$ on both datasets. It shrinks from $-0.263$ to $-0.068$ on TUAB and from $-0.218$ to $-0.037$ on CAUEEG. This argues that anchoring the concept supervision helps in reducing intervention gap. However, the gap does not close to zero at either $\lambda$ value on both dataset ($-0.068$ TUAB, $-0.037$ CAUEEG at $\lambda=20$). 

On the other hand, the accuracy cost of this mitigation differs sharply by dataset. On TUAB, $\lambda=5$ accuracy actually rises slightly ($0.827 \to 0.842$) alongside the gains in $R^2$ and smaller intervention gap. With $\lambda=20$ accuracy decreased relative to the $\lambda=0.5$ baseline ($0.827 \to 0.820$). On CAUEEG, by contrast, both new $\lambda$ values has substantial decrease accuracy relative to the $\lambda=0.5$ baseline. The accuracy decreased from $0.579$  to $0.517$ at $\lambda=5$ and further to $0.506$ at $\lambda=20$. 



\section{Discussion}

The main finding of this study is the introduction of a clinical concept bottleneck in the domain of EEG evaluated in both a hybrid form (with a residual pathway) and a pure form (without one). 

\subsection{Bottleneck models}

We used three seeds to train all three TUAB models. The models achieved between $0.827$ and $0.836$ in the classification task using TUAB dataset. Several Deep Learning models have been applied on the TUAB dataset and achieved accuracy between $0.779$ and $0.866$ depending on the model size and architecture type \cite{kiessner2023extended}. Using the CAUEEG dataset, The accuracies are between $0.579$ and $0.627$. The previous results from the original authors of the dataset \cite{kim2023caueeg} achieved accuracy is $0.543$ using a simple CNN model. So, we argue that the accuracies from the two datasets align with what is reported on these two benchmarks.

Across the three seeds, both hybrid and pure match the black-box CNN baseline on TUAB and CAUEEG have slightly lower reduction in performance. The reduction in accuracy is $0.9$ and $0.7$ percentage points in the TUAB dataset for both pure and hybrid models. On the CAUEEG dataset, The reduction in accuracy is $4.8$ and $2.2$ percentage points. In the CBMs literature, it is shown that accuracy reduction is an expected cost of concept interpretability \cite{koh2020, mahinpei2021}. 

However, the performance does not mean the two hybrid and pure modes are equivalent as explanations. Further analyses in this paper show the hybrid model accuracy is not really coming from its concepts. First, the leakage analysis (section~\ref{sec:leakage}) shows that the hybrid residual pathway carries substantial independent signal on both datasets. The linear probe given only the residual features, with no access to the predicted concepts, recovers $81.9\%$ accuracy on TUAB and $63.6\%$ on CAUEEG. 


\subsection{Concept Quality and Concept Intervention}

The concept quality is good indicator as only concepts that are predicted reliably should be presented as explanations to clinicians. Concept prediction quality varies strongly across concept groups (table~\ref{tab:r2family}). Band power concepts are the most reliably predicted in every configuration. Concepts such as spectral ratios and alpha peak amplitude are also predicted well under the hybrid design. In contrast, the
hemispheric asymmetry concepts are not learned in any configuration, and PLV connectivity is only partly learnable on TUAB and not on CAUEEG. The values of $R^2$ increased significantly once the $\lambda$ increased which means that concepts are more reliably predicted. This is highlighted in details in table~\ref{tab:lambda_sweep}.


The intervention experiment (section~\ref{sec:intervention}) separates the two designs. Replacing all $30$ predicted concepts with their true values leaves the hybrid's accuracy essentially unchanged on both datasets ($+0.002$ on TUAB, $-0.006$ on CAUEEG). Removing the residual pathway lowers concept quality in every family on both datasets, while accuracy changes little. This is consistent with its concept predictions being relatively close to their true values as highlighted by the values of $R^2$. The pure bottleneck behaves differently. the same intervention collapses the accuracy on both datasets ($-0.263$ on TUAB, $-0.218$ on CAUEEG). the pure classifier rely on its own predicted concept values, which deviate from the true values which is highlighted in its lower concept $R^2$ as explained in section~\ref{sec:concept_quality}. The leakage is aimed to be solved by increasing the concept-loss weight $\lambda$ which helped to increase $R^2$ substantially and reduce concept intervention accuracies. The mitigation is explained in section~\ref{sec:mitigation}. The mitigation strategy has a small accuracy cost on TUAB and substantial cost on CAUEEG. The choice of design is therefore a trade-off between concept faithfulness and classification accuracy.



\subsection{Concept leakage and mitigation}
\label{sec:discussion_leakage}

The pure bottleneck reaches accuracy close to the hybrid model. However, its concepts are markedly less faithful (table~\ref{tab:r2family}), and replacing them with their true values collapses its accuracy (table~\ref{tab:intervention}). This is known as concept leakage \cite{mahinpei2021, havasi2022}. Without a residual pathway, the classification loss pulls the predicted concepts away from their analytical values so that they carry hidden label information. The learning is not relevant to the true concepts. The hybrid is less affected because its residual pathway offers an alternative path for encoding the task relevant patterns. Accuracy alone therefore cannot reveal whether concept explanations are faithful.


Arguably, increasing the weight of the concept loss $\lambda$ substantially reduces this leakage. Concept $R^2$ rises and the intervention gap shrinks on both datasets (table~\ref{tab:lambda_sweep}). However, a gap of $3.7$--$6.8$ points remains at $\lambda = 20$. Furthermore, some concepts such as the asymmetry concepts stay unlearnable. This migiation comes with accuracy cost that reflects how completely the concept set covers each task. It is small on TUAB and substantial on CAUEEG. 


\subsection{Limitations}

The $30$ candidate concepts do not cover all pathologically relevant EEG features for the abnormal pathologies. Many candidate concepts can be proposed here including additional and systematic functional connectivity concepts, network features such as node degrees, global and local efficiency, clustering coefficient and complexity \cite{10.1007/978-981-95-9575-4_2}. As EEG has been extensively used in neurological research, a comprehensive list of candidate biomarkers can be implemented here which could be a future direction. Further experimental settings including different models based on sequence, attentions and graph based models can be explored here.  

Section~\ref{sec:mitigation} tests one possible fix, but only for the pure model own concept problem (raising $\lambda$, the concept-loss weight). This fix does not apply to the hybrid residual-pathway leakage specifically, and we did not retrain the hybrid under larger $\lambda$ to check whether it would improve concept $R^2$ there too without reducing leakage. A possible direct fix for the hybrid's own leakage remains future work, such as \cite{galliamov2026conceptsinformationbottleneckmodels} which integrates a penalization or regularization layer into the model. This penalty applied specifically to the residual own contribution to the classifier.

In this study, results are reported on two datasets (TUAB, CAUEEG) spanning different pathologies and age ranges. Further validation on additional corpora for certain pathologies such as Dementia and Alzheimer would strengthen generalization claims in this study. Due to the limited availability of datasets that are suitable for training the models, this remains a less viable option to extend this study. The results are not explained in lights of clinical and neuroscientific biomarkers. A detailed neurophysiological interpretation of individual concept contributions is beyond the scope of this study. This is left as a future direction to limit the scope of this article. 


\section{Conclusion}
We have presented concept bottleneck models (CBMs) for EEG pathology detection, in a hybrid form (CB-CNN, with a residual pathway) and a pure form (CB-CNN-Plain, without one). Predictions are routed through 30 clinical EEG biomarkers computed analytically from the signal, so the concepts require no manual annotation and can be inspected and corrected by clinicians at inference time.

Across three seeds, both variants achieve similar accuracy to the black-box CNN on abnormality detection (TUAB) and are two to five percentage points lower on dementia classification (CAUEEG). However, similar accuracy does not imply faithful explanations. The pure bottleneck has symptoms of concept leakage. The predicted concepts deviate from their analytical values (mean $R^2$ of $-0.213$ on TUAB and $0.026$ on CAUEEG). Replacing  them with their true values reduces the accuracy by $26$ and $22$ percentage points. The hybrid model remains unaffected by the concept intervention. Linear probes show that its concepts are sufficient for abnormality detection, whereas on the dementia task its residual pathway carries information that the concept set does not contain.

Increasing the weight of concept loss substantially reduces concept leakage in the pure bottleneck. Using $\lambda = 20$, mean concept $R^2$ rises to $0.533$ on TUAB and $0.639$ on CAUEEG. The intervention gap shrinks to $7$ and $4$ percentage points. The accuracy cost of this mitigation is small on TUAB but larger on CAUEEG. This reflects that concept prediction quality and intervention should be reported along with the accuracy when concept bottlenecks are used for clinical settings.

Future work includes extending the concept set with additional connectivity and network features. Evaluating alternative Leakage mitigation strategies such as regularisation of the residual pathway. This study would benefits from validating the approach on further clinical corpora and with detailed neurophysiological interpretation.
